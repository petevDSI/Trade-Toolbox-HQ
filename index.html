How the numbers were built. Read this before you buy.

The labor hours and part costs in the task library are starting estimates, not industry-standard figures. We wrote them ourselves with AI assistance and refined them by averaging two review passes. They were not copied from any commercial flat-rate guide, and they have not yet been verified by a working technician. A technician review is the next step, and this page will say so when it happens.

Some of the defaults do come from published sources: the technician wage and payroll burden follow U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data, and the diagnostic-fee range follows a 2026 pricing guide from Housecall Pro. That guide is itself a benchmark built from consumer pricing published by Angi, TaskRabbit, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack, so it shows what homeowners commonly pay, not what any one shop should charge. Other defaults, including the shop rate, overhead and the add-on percentages, are placeholders we chose. Replace them with your own costs.

What changes your price. No national average fits every shop. Check these before you quote:

  • Local labor rates and cost of living
  • Appliance type and brand tier (standard versus luxury or built-in)
  • Gas versus electric, since gas repairs often cost more
  • Part availability and appliance age
  • Whether the diagnostic fee is credited toward the repair
  • Warranty status and whether a home warranty company is paying

The 50% target margin is a rule of thumb, not a verified benchmark. Published figures vary, so set it from your own books.

Treat every price as a planning aid. Check each repair against your own parts costs and your own time, and adjust the yellow cells. The workbook is built so you can.
